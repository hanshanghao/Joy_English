Warm-up · Hitting and Spelling

5 min

Let's warm up with a spelling game! Look at the picture, then hit the letters in the right order to spell the word.

👩‍🏫 Teacher instructions

  • Put the Warm-up picture cards on the board. Words are chosen at random from a pool — duplicate cards are possible.
  • Have a student take a picture card and say the word aloud.
  • Two teams race to spell it by tapping the letter tiles in order. First team to spell it correctly scores. (Example spelling method from the manual: h-i-t for "hit".)
  • Mime the animal too — for example, act like a duck to review duck and cow from Unit 11.

Lesson Objectives

by the end of class

Speaking & Listening

  • Name farm animals: goat, horse, chicken, cow, duck, pig.
  • Say what a group is: We are goats.
  • Say how the animals feel: We are not happy.
  • Ask and answer Who are we?

Vocabulary & Patterns

  • Use singular and plural: goat → goats.
  • Use the contraction: We are → We're, are not → aren't.
  • Listen and respond to the farm-animal story.
  • Show care for animals (Tales of Values).

The Unhappy Farm Animals

Dialogue · 15 min

Tap any animal on the farm to hear what it says. The animals are not happy — can you find out why?

👩‍🏫 Before you read

  • Pre-teach: farm, animals, unhappy / not happy, field, barn, mud.
  • Play the dialogue once with books closed. Ask: Which animals can you hear?
  • Point at each speaker as you play the line so students connect voice → animal.

A Who Says What? · match the speaker to the line

Click a line, then click the animal that says it.

B Mystery Detective · Who are we?

Read the clue, then choose the animal group.

C Role-play · the whole class farm

Split the class into groups. Each group is one animal. Read your line together — then perform the ending!

Farm-Animal Words

Vocabulary · 10 min

Tap a card to hear the word. Tap again to reveal the plural. New words: goat, horse, chicken. Review: cow, duck, pig.

👩‍🏫 Teaching steps

  • Show each picture, say the word twice, students repeat (1-2-3 chorus).
  • Play a listen & point game: "Point to the goats!" Students point to the plural picture.
  • Board display: write the singular and plural side by side. Circle the -s.

A Singular or Plural?

Look at the picture. Is it one animal or many? Choose the right word.

Pattern · We are / We're

Patterns · 20 min
We are goats.  =  We're goats.
We are not happy.  =  We aren't happy.
We are→We're are not→aren't

👩‍🏫 Board display & drill

  • Write: We are goats. → We're goats. Underline that We're = We are.
  • Whole-class chain drill: change the animal each time — We're cows. We're ducks. We're chickens.
  • Add feelings: We're happy. We're not happy. We aren't hungry.

Teacher Guide · 50-minute plan

printable
5 min
Warm-up · Hitting & Spelling
15 min
Dialogue · The Unhappy Farm Animals
10 min
Vocabulary · farm animals
20 min
Patterns · We are / We're + games
1 · Warm-up (5 min)

Picture-card spelling race. Students take a card, say the word, and two teams race to spell it with the tiles. Review duck and cow. Award points on the scoreboard.

2 · Dialogue (15 min)

Pre-teach key words. Play the dialogue once (books closed). Then read line by line, pointing at each animal. Finish with Who Says What?, Mystery Detective and the class role-play.

3 · Vocabulary (10 min)

New words: goat, horse, chicken. Review: cow, duck, pig. Chorus repeat, then Listen & Point, then Singular or Plural and Mime & Guess.

4 · Patterns (20 min)

Board display: We are goats. → We're goats. and are not → aren't. Chain drill, substitution drill, contraction builder, build-the-sentence, fix-the-sentence, then Who Are We? and pattern talk.

5 · Game Zone & wrap-up (as time allows)

Play the ten Game Zone activities. Use the timer for each round. End with the Exit Ticket and the Tales of Values discussion.

6 · Differentiation
  • Support: give the first letter of a word; use only singular forms first.
  • Challenge: add feelings and numbers — We're six hungry goats!
  • Movement: the Mime & Guess and role-play games get students out of their seats.
