Preparation notes

Practical reminders from the Teacher's Manual.

Make the adjectives concrete. Act them out while you describe them. Use pictures of a famous wealthy person for rich, and the Scarecrow from Tales of Joy 1 (tattered clothing) for poor. For excited, show a favourite celebrity or party invitation. For bored, sit down, sigh, and drum your fingers.
Say "poor" carefully. Model poor as /pʊə/ (it rhymes with tour in this lesson). Do not let it drift to the long /pɔː/ sound.
Activity Book C: opposites. The opposites exercise may be difficult. Do a few examples together in class before students complete it independently. The Opposites Match game in the Vocabulary tab gives extra practice.
Return Unit 11 quizzes. Students correct them during the break, staple them into their Contact Books, and show their parents at home.

Story background

For teacher reference, not for memorisation.

The giant returns to the castle and sits for a while. He becomes bored and tells his wife to bring out his magic hen. Jack peeks out from behind the table and tells his friends that the magic hen belongs to his father. His friends ask what it can do. Jack explains that it can lay golden eggs. Right after he says so, the hen lays a beautiful golden egg. What will Jack do next? The story continues in Unit 14.

Key language
I'm bored. Give me my magic hen! That's my father's magic hen! It can lay golden eggs.
Note on the supplied dialogue: the speaker of "What can it do?" is not named in the supplied dialogue text. It is shown in this lesson as Friend, and it is clearly labelled as an unidentified speaker in the role-play. No official attribution is invented.

Teacher's Manual support

Story-telling steps and the background story from the manual.

Scene 1 - present the demand

Put on the giant's headband, sit in a chair, yawn, stand up, rub your chin and pace. Say: "I'm bored. Give me my magic hen!" Invite a volunteer to hand you the hen picture and say: "Here you are." Applaud the volunteer.

Scene 2 - Jack's discovery

Put on Jack's headband, hide behind a table, peek out and act shocked: "That's my father's magic hen!" Ask the class what it can do. Celebrate "golden eggs". Ask: "What sound does a hen make?" The class answers: "Cluck! Cluck!"

Teacher's Manual vocabulary routine

Simple English, actions and expressions.

Excited: "The boy is watching a game. He is excited." Make an ecstatic face and say, "Oh yeah! I'm excited."
Bored: Sit, lean on the table, put your chin in your hands, sigh and yawn. "No homework. No computer. I have nothing to do. I am so bored."
Rich: Show a photo of a wealthy person. "He has a lot of money. He can buy a castle, an airplane and many cars. He is rich."
Poor: Show Jack turning out his empty pockets. "Jack has no money. He is poor." Point at the girl on the flashcard: "She has no money for new clothes."

Manual pattern routine

Mechanical practice, acting, and the finding game.

Be-verb warm-up: say a subject, for example "I", and toss a beach ball. The student who catches it says "I am". Repeat with he, she, you.
Act and infer: "I am going to a zoo this Sunday. I am excited." Throw up your hands. Students point at you: "You are excited." Then act bored with a broken computer.
Finding game: write six situation strips. Roll the die, call a number, and the student reads the matching strip and says the pattern, for example: "I have no money." → "I am poor."

Teacher script

Warm-up (5 minutes)
Review be-verbs with a beach ball. Say a subject and toss the ball. The catcher says the matching be-verb: "I" → "am", "he" → "is", "she" → "is". Repeat quickly.
Dialogue (15 minutes)

Story lead-in: "Do you remember? The giant is in the castle. He can smell Nick, Jack and Abby. What did his wife say about the smell?" Students: "Don't be silly. That's just the cake."

Scene 1: Put on the giant's headband. Yawn, stand, rub your chin, pace. "I'm bored. Give me my magic hen!" A volunteer hands over the hen: "Here you are." Applaud.

Scene 2: Put on Jack's headband. Hide behind the table, peek out, act shocked: "That's my father's magic hen!" Ask what the hen can do. Celebrate: "It can lay golden eggs." Ask for the hen sound: "Cluck! Cluck!"

Giant: "I'm bored. Give me my magic hen!"
Giantess: "Here you are."
Jack: "That's my father's magic hen!"
Friend: "What can it do?"
Magic Hen: "It can lay golden eggs."

Role-play: point at the lines, use character voices, divide the class into groups, switch roles. Encourage fluency.

Vocabulary (15 minutes)

Use simple English, actions and expressions. Present each word with a scenario. Then show the picture side and say the word three times. Flip to the word side and sound out and spell: "P-o-o-r, poor."

Spin the Wheel: divide the board into four sections, put one flashcard in each. Spin, call a number. The student stands, says the word, sits. The team spells and says the word. Correct team +1; fastest team +1.

Patterns (15 minutes)

Warm-up: "I" → "I am"; "he" → "he is"; "she" → "she is".

Present: "I am going to a zoo this Sunday. I am excited." Throw up your hands. Students: "You are excited." Then: "My computer is broken. I am bored." Students: "You are bored."

Write: I am bored. Substitute the adjective flashcard. Pass flashcards to students; each stands and says "I am excited." The class points and says "You are excited." Then "He is excited." / "She is excited."

Activities + Review (15 minutes)
Use the Listen, Look & Say activity and the Listen, Point & Say activity. Run a quick review of the four words and the three subject patterns. Use the class repetition counter and the point-and-say team game.
Vocabulary & Patterns / Puzzles (15 minutes)

Do the Puzzles & Write: four groups; number students. One student from each team completes the puzzle while teammates discuss. Then groups write the answer using the Writing Relay. Tap Answer to reveal.

Use Auto Sound, Random or Sequential mode. Edit the practice bank if you want your own sentences.

Phonics Quiz + Contact Book (15 minutes)
Use your phonics programme quiz. Review the Unit 13 sound check. Hand back corrected Unit 11 quizzes; students correct them, staple them into Contact Books and show parents at home.
Difficulties and homework

Poor: /pʊə/; keep the vowel short and do not drift to the long "paw" vowel.

Activity Book C: opposites may be difficult. Do examples together before independent work.

Homework: listen, read, write, memorise, point and say, record. Correct the Unit 11 quiz and sign the Contact Book.
